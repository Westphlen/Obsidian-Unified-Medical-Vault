### Where Do I Even Begin?

Perhaps it was that afternoon.

I stumbled upon a video by content creator Menghu Kuangben (Tiger Sprint), where he introduced his medical root and affix database. As the upbeat BGM played, bidirectional links wove themselves into a dense web, exuding an inexplicable sense of tension and technological wonder. It was absolutely captivating.

This free knowledge base for medical students opened the door to the world of Obsidian for me. That year, I was a freshman, busily strategically mapping out my college life.

### The Beginning

To be honest, the beginning was anything but smooth. Obsidian is renowned for its vast plugin ecosystem and unparalleled customization freedom. I once immersed myself in the PKMer community and downloaded over fifty plugins in one go (though I still occasionally go treasure-hunting there now). Yet my notes remained rudimentary.

Freshman year was all about general education courses. Editing LaTeX formulas for college physics, organic chemistry, and other science subjects left me completely overwhelmed. The note structure I designed to mimic textbook tables of contents made searching for knowledge points inconvenient. And with my two-finger typing... God, it was painfully slow.

### Luhmann and Touch Typing

That all changed when I watched a video about Luhmann's Zettelkasten method. Atomic notes have since become the foundational logic of this knowledge base. I strive to bridge the last mile of tiny, interconnected knowledge points (like the pontine tegmentum syndrome in the bottom-right corner of the image), ensuring each file conveys simple, clear information.

Theory is nothing without practice. I spent an entire afternoon before the student union team-building event learning touch typing from scratch. With electronic textbooks at hand, I began systematically organizing, formatting, and importing massive amounts of notes into the knowledge base.

### Standardization

However, at that time, I tried to use every single feature Obsidian offered all at once. Tags, italics, annotations, and dividers were everywhere, with no consistent system—my pages were an absolute mess.

Gradually, I phased out excessive tags and annotations and settled on my current format:
I use WorkFlowy-style outline notes to build "property-content" key-value pairs, and the EasyTyping plugin to bold property names.

Yes, that's how simple a note's format is. I believe simplicity is the ultimate sophistication.

### Confusion and Breakthrough

To be honest, as the AI wave hit, I increasingly doubted the value of this knowledge base.

"Why bother making notes manually when you can just ask AI? And your notes might even have mistakes."
"Besides, what's the difference between your knowledge base and Wikipedia? Why not just look it up there?"

Caught between AI and Wikipedia, I didn't know what purpose it served.

That is, until I accidentally pressed Ctrl+H. A block of text was highlighted.

And then I understood its unique value: it's yours.

AI generates content quickly, but it's content you've never seen before, with a high cognitive cost to understand. When you search in Obsidian, however, everything comes flooding back instantly—you can read quickly and grasp the key points immediately.

The same logic applies to Wikipedia. Personalization and customization are everything. You can add or delete content at any time, inject your own understanding, use your preferred formatting, and add handy, informal mnemonics...

### The Bridge

So, what use is this knowledge base to others? You might say, "Well, this is your database. We've never seen it before. Isn't reading it just as slow as comprehending AI or Wikipedia output?"

Perhaps not. This database is a rearrangement of People's Medical Publishing House (PMPH) textbooks, with a small amount of personal interpretation—mostly annotations I made during final exam review. So you might actually find it surprisingly familiar.

The textbook serves as the bridge between you and this knowledge base. On this foundation, you can build your own intellectual edifice.

### Imagination

This database holds infinite possibilities. Obsidian's rich plugin ecosystem gives it wings. For example:

• Cloze can convert all highlighted text blocks into fill-in-the-blank questions

• MindmapNextGen can turn outlines into mind maps with one click, just like WorkFlowy

• Cards View displays notes as cards in a waterfall flow

• Notebook Navigator, paired with the aliases property, turns notes into flashcards for medical English vocabulary memorization

Ironically, the flashiest feature—the bidirectional links—often only provide emotional value. Of course, without that emotional value, I would never have kept going.

In short, use your imagination to unlock its full potential!
